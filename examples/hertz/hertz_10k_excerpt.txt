THE HERTZ CORPORATION
FORM 10-K EXCERPT (FY 2019)

ITEM 1. BUSINESS

The Hertz Corporation, through its subsidiaries, operates one of the world's largest vehicle rental businesses. As of December 31, 2019, the Company operated approximately 12,400 corporate and franchisee locations in approximately 150 countries worldwide.

BUSINESS SEGMENTS

The Company operates through two reportable segments:

1. Americas Rental Car ("Americas RAC"): Vehicle rental operations in North America, Central America, South America, and the Caribbean.

2. International Rental Car ("International RAC"): Vehicle rental operations in Europe, Middle East, Africa, Asia, and Australasia.

FLEET

As of December 31, 2019:
- Americas RAC Fleet: Approximately 450,000 vehicles
- International RAC Fleet: Approximately 250,000 vehicles
- Total Fleet: Approximately 700,000 vehicles

Average age of rental fleet: 18 months
Fleet mix: 80% sedans/SUVs, 20% commercial trucks/vans

ITEM 7. MANAGEMENT'S DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS

CRITICAL ACCOUNTING POLICIES

Fleet Assets and Depreciation:
- Vehicles held for rental are stated at cost, net of accumulated depreciation and allowances for impairment.
- Depreciation is calculated using the straight-line method over the estimated useful life of the asset (12-24 months for rental fleet).
- Residual value assumptions: 45-55% of original vehicle cost at time of sale.

Impairment Risk: If used vehicle prices decline significantly, the Company may be required to record impairment charges on its rental fleet. A 10% decline in residual values would result in approximately $1.5 billion of incremental depreciation and impairment expense.

RISK FACTORS

COVID-19 Pandemic Risk [NOTE: This 10-K was filed in February 2020, pre-bankruptcy]:

In early 2020, the novel coronavirus (COVID-19) began spreading globally, resulting in travel restrictions, quarantines, and reduced economic activity. The extent of the impact on the Company's business is uncertain and depends on factors including:
- Duration and severity of the pandemic
- Government travel restrictions and border closures
- Decline in business and leisure travel demand
- Impact on airline capacity and ridesharing alternatives

If travel demand declines significantly for an extended period, the Company's revenues, profitability, and liquidity would be materially adversely affected. Additionally, a sharp decline in used vehicle prices could require significant impairment charges on the rental fleet.

Used Vehicle Residual Value Risk:

The Company's profitability depends significantly on its ability to sell used vehicles at anticipated residual values. Factors affecting used vehicle prices include:
- New vehicle incentives and pricing
- Economic conditions and consumer credit availability
- Supply of off-lease vehicles entering the used car market
- Fuel prices and consumer preferences

A 5% decline in average residual values would reduce annual pre-tax income by approximately $750 million.
A 10% decline would reduce annual pre-tax income by approximately $1,500 million.

Given 2019 pre-tax income of $375 million, a 10% residual value decline would result in a loss of over $1 billion.

Leverage and Covenant Compliance Risk:

As of December 31, 2019, the Company's consolidated leverage ratio was 5.2:1.0, approaching the covenant maximum of 6.0:1.0. If operating performance deteriorates:
- Leverage ratio could exceed 6.0:1.0, triggering default
- Interest coverage could fall below 2.5:1.0, triggering default
- Liquidity could fall below $500 million minimum

Covenant default would permit lenders to accelerate debt repayment and/or terminate credit facilities, which could force the Company into bankruptcy.

Liquidity Risk:

The Company's asset-backed credit facility is subject to borrowing base calculations tied to rental fleet values. If fleet values decline (due to used car price deterioration), the borrowing base would shrink, requiring immediate repayment of any excess outstanding debt. This could create a liquidity crisis if the Company is unable to refinance or raise alternative capital.

- COVID-19 pandemic causes near-total collapse in travel demand
- Rental car revenues decline 90%+ in April 2020

- The Hertz Corporation and certain subsidiaries file voluntary petitions for relief under Chapter 11 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code
- Debtor-in-possession (DIP) financing: $1 billion facility approved

- Hertz equity trades at $2-6 per share on retail speculation (despite bankruptcy filing)
- Company proposes (and later withdraws) plan to issue new stock to retail investors
- Credit markets debate enterprise value: $7B (liquidation) vs. $13B+ (optimistic going concern)

June 2021:
- Hertz emerges from bankruptcy under new ownership (Knighthead Capital, Certares Opportunity Fund)
- Old equity cancelled (worthless)
- Creditor recoveries: First lien (100%), Second lien (converted to new equity), Unsecured (~75%)

FINANCIAL COVENANT DEFINITIONS

Consolidated Leverage Ratio:
= Consolidated Total Debt / Consolidated EBITDA (trailing 12 months)

Where:
- Consolidated Total Debt = All borrowed money including capital leases (net of unrestricted cash >$100M)
- Consolidated EBITDA = Net Income + Interest + Taxes + Depreciation + Amortization + [Permitted Add-Backs]

Permitted Add-Backs:
1. Non-cash charges (stock compensation, impairments, write-downs)
2. Restructuring charges (up to $150M annually)
3. Non-recurring items (litigation settlements, transaction costs)
4. Run-rate cost savings from restructuring (up to $100M annually, if documented)

Consolidated Interest Coverage Ratio:
= Consolidated EBITDA / Consolidated Interest Expense (trailing 12 months)

Where:
- Consolidated Interest Expense = Cash interest paid (excluding capitalized interest and non-cash PIK interest)

DEBT PRIORITY AND COLLATERAL (DETAILED)

Priority Waterfall (in event of default or bankruptcy):

1. DIP Financing (if applicable in bankruptcy): Super-super-priority
   - Primes all pre-petition debt
   - First claim on all assets

2. Asset-Backed First Lien (Fleet Financing): First priority on fleet
   - Secured by rental car fleet (700,000 vehicles)
   - Borrowing base tied to fleet appraised value
   - Entitled to adequate protection in bankruptcy (replacement liens, cash payments)

3. Corporate Second Lien: Second priority on fleet; first priority on non-fleet assets
   - Second lien on rental fleet (behind asset-backed lenders)
   - First lien on: real estate, furniture/fixtures, intellectual property, equity in subsidiaries
   - Intercreditor agreement subordinates to asset-backed lenders on fleet

4. Senior Unsecured Notes: Unsecured claims
   - No collateral
   - Pari passu with trade creditors, lease rejection claims, other unsecured obligations
   - Structurally subordinated to debt of non-guarantor subsidiaries

5. Subordinated Debt (if any): Junior unsecured

6. Equity: Residual interest (last in priority)

KEY RISKS AND RED FLAGS (Signal Triage Detection)

1. COVENANT HEADROOM:
   - Leverage ratio: 5.2x (max 6.0x) → Only 0.8x cushion
   - Interest coverage: 3.1x (min 2.5x) → Only 0.6x cushion
   - Risk: Any operating deterioration likely triggers default

2. RESIDUAL VALUE EXPOSURE:
   - 10% decline in used car prices → $1.5B loss (vs. $285M net income)
   - March-April 2020 actual decline: 25-30%
   - Result: Massive losses, covenant breaches

3. LIQUIDITY STRESS:
   - Asset-backed borrowing base tied to fleet value
   - Fleet value declines → Borrowing base shrinks → Forced repayment
   - COVID-19 impact: Fleet value declined 20-30%, triggering liquidity crisis

4. STRUCTURAL SUBORDINATION:
   - Unsecured noteholders have no claim on fleet (80%+ of asset value)
   - Dependent on going concern value of non-fleet assets
   - Risk: Liquidation wipes out unsecured recovery

5. FULCRUM IDENTIFICATION:
   - At $9B EV: Fulcrum = Second Lien (last class paid in full)
   - At $11.5B EV: Fulcrum = Unsecured (partial recovery)
   - At $13.5B+ EV: Fulcrum = Equity (only if all creditors paid in full)
